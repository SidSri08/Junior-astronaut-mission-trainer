# Simulation Assumptions & Simplifications (D-S3)

**Owner:** Department D (D1 NASA Scientific Research Analyst)  
**Contract Reference:** `src/data/gameBalance.js` (`label: 'SIMULATION_VALUE'`) & `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md`

To make **Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer** playable, fair, and understandable for children aged 7–12 in a 7-minute browser session, all gameplay rates, capacities, and timers in `src/data/gameBalance.js` are tagged **`SIMULATION_VALUE`**. This document records how each simulation mechanic simplifies real lunar outpost engineering (**`REAL_SCIENTIFIC_FACT`**).

## 1. Master Comparison Table: `SIMULATION_VALUE` vs. Real Lunar Science

| System / Parameter | In-Game `SIMULATION_VALUE` (`gameBalance.js`) | Real Lunar Engineering / Science (`REAL_SCIENTIFIC_FACT`) | Pedagogical Reason for Simplification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mission Day Length** | `dayLengthSeconds: 60` (1 mission day = 60 real seconds; 7 days = 7 minutes) | ~29.53 Earth days per lunar synodic cycle (~709 hours from sunrise to sunrise). | Lets young players complete a full 7-day mission arc within a single 7–10 minute classroom or home session. |
| **South-Pole Daylight Cycle** | `daylightFraction: 0.8` per 60 s day (48 s sunlight, 12 s night/shadow every day) + `longShadow` event (28 s) | High-elevation peaks at the lunar south pole (e.g., Shackleton rim) receive sunlight for 70–90% of the lunar year, interrupted by multi-day terrain shadows. | Teaches the *concept* of polar solar availability and battery budgeting during regular shadow periods without waiting days. |
| **Resource Units (O₂, H₂O, Food, Battery)** | Normalized `0..100` percentage-like game units | Measured in physical units: kg of $\text{O}_2$ (~0.84 kg/person/day), liters/kg of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, kcal/kg of food, and kWh of electrical energy. | Kids aged 7–12 easily compare 0–100 bars across systems without converting between kilograms, liters, kilocalories, and kilowatt-hours. |
| **Crew Size** | `crew.count: 4` astronauts | Early Artemis surface missions land 2 astronauts on the surface (with 4 in Orion/Gateway), scaling to 4+ in later surface habitat phases. | Matches the 4-person Artemis crew size while representing a full small research team. |
| **Oxygen Generation & Water Coupling** | Life Support uses `0.12` water/s to produce `0.7` O₂/s (`waterForOxygen`, `oxygenMake`) while crew uses `0.45` O₂/s | ISS Oxygen Generation Assembly electrolyzes water ($2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2$); 1 kg of water yields ~0.89 kg of $\text{O}_2$, enough for ~1 crew-day. | Preserves the true causal link—*making oxygen consumes water and electricity*—while scaling rates to a 60-second day. |
| **Water Recycling Efficiency** | Life Support recycles `0.16` water/s vs. `0.05` crew drinking + `0.12` electrolysis use (net gradual loss requiring rover ice runs) | ISS ECLSS recovers ~93–98% of crew wastewater and cabin humidity condensate; losses come from brine, airlock vents, and H₂ venting. | Shows that recycling stretches water dramatically, yet closed loops aren't 100% lossless—motivating polar ice harvesting (ISRU). |
| **Rover Ice Harvesting (`collectIce`)** | Instant button action (`-10` battery, `+25` water, `40 s` cooldown) | Mining PSR water ice requires driving into ~40–100 K cryogenic craters, excavating hard icy regolith, heating/sublimating volatiles, and filtering contaminants. | Teaches that polar craters contain harvestable water ice and that rover trips cost energy, without complex thermal-mining UI. |
| **Crop Growth & Harvest (`greenhouse`)** | `0 -> 100%` growth in ~90 simulated seconds (`1.1%/s`), yielding `+30` food | Fast space crops like red romaine lettuce or radishes take ~28–35 Earth days to mature; fruiting crops like dwarf peppers take 90–120 days. | Allows players to plant, tend, and harvest multiple crop cycles during a 7-day mission so they experience bioregenerative food production. |
| **Radiation Dose & Recovery** | `0..100` bar (`background: 0.03/s`, `stormUnsheltered: 1.6/s`, `stormSheltered: 0.12/s`, slow `recoveryPerSecond: 0.01`) | Measured in millisieverts (mSv). Lunar surface background GCR is ~1.1–1.4 mSv/day (~380–500 mSv/year); large SPEs can deliver 100–1,000+ mSv without shelter. Ionizing radiation DNA damage is largely cumulative. | The 0–100 hazard meter makes invisible radiation tangible for kids; sheltering reduces storm dose by >90%, and regolith shielding (`shieldLevel 1..3`) cuts rate by 22% per level. |
| **Regolith Shielding (`buildShielding`)** | 3 discrete upgrade levels (`-15` battery, `-1` repair kit, `-22%` radiation per level) | Robotic excavators or 3D-sintering systems pile ~0.5–3 meters of regolith over habitats over days or weeks to increase areal density ($\text{g/cm}^2$). | Visualizes regolith berms/arches around the habitat in 3D and rewards proactive ISRU protection. |
| **Equipment Wear & Repair Kits** | Continuous wear (`0.02–0.025/s`) fixed with generic `repairKits` (`+40` health) | Dust abrasion, thermal cycling, and mechanical fatigue require specialized spare parts, filters, seals, and scheduled crew maintenance hours. | Abstracts spare-parts logistics into an intuitive "Repair Kit" inventory item so kids learn that Moon bases cannot easily buy parts from Earth. |
| **Minigame 1: Leak Hunter** | Warm/cold pressure sensor cursor over hull grid; tap 3 leaks before timer expires | Astronauts isolate leaking modules, read pressure transducers, and sweep hull seams with ultrasonic leak detectors before applying patches/sealant. | Translates sensor-guided troubleshooting into an engaging 20-second spatial search game. |
| **Minigame 2: Solar Aligner** | Drag slider to match Sun angle ($\cos\theta$ output) and brush dust particles across 3 rounds | Motor-driven solar gimbals track the Sun automatically, while dust mitigation uses electrodynamic dust shields (EDS), coatings, or mechanical brushing. | Gives hands-on intuition for cosine angle loss and dust shading on solar panels. |
| **Mission Loss Condition** | Crew health reaches `0` → *"Mission Control brings the crew home safely"* | Life-support failure or severe radiation exposure on the Moon is life-threatening; abort return to Earth takes ~3–5 days. | Keeps the game age-appropriate and non-violent for 7–12 year olds while clearly explaining which vital system failed and why. |

## 2. How the UI Communicates This Distinction
1. **Explicit Labeling:** Every fact card pulled from `src/data/scienceFacts.js` is badged as a **Real NASA Science Fact** (`REAL_SCIENTIFIC_FACT`) with its source ID(s).
2. **No False Engineering Numbers:** Tutorials, tooltips, and event lessons explain *physical cause-and-effect relationships* (e.g., "Splitting water makes oxygen," "Dust blocks sunlight," "Regolith blocks radiation") rather than claiming that `0.7/s` or `60-second days` are real NASA specifications.
